# D-0029: The weapons workbench, and the lock-on chip

A player asked, through Rusty, on 2026-10-03: "Make the rocket launcher have a consumable craftable
item ie circuit board or something similar to be able to use lock in feature. If no lock on chip
installed only point and shoot available. Have the rocket launcher have similar menu as magazines
for loading. However this would be where you install chip."

Rusty, asked how the chip's screen should open: "I was actually flirting with the idea of a new
crafting type block called the weapons workbench, which is where you do all gun related assembly
and modification (no longer at the crafting station). Maybe we fold that into here?"

His calls:
- **The chip wears per guided shot.** It holds a number of guided launches, then burns out.
- **The bench takes guns, parts, magazines and chips.** Rounds and rockets stay at the crafting
  table, so the warehouse can still craft ammunition in bulk.
- **The art is ours.** Astra is no longer part of the work: "All you buddy I got rid of astra".

## The decision

**The weapons workbench is a block whose screen is a crafting grid with its own recipe type, and
a fitting panel.**
- **The block,** `rangedweaponsmod:weapons_workbench`:
  - a full cube in the smithing table's family, built, broken and burnt as one;
  - its front, with a rifle and a pistol on pegs, faces whoever placed it;
  - made at the crafting table from three steel over a plank, a crafting table and a plank, over
    three planks. Holding steel reveals it in the recipe book.
- **Its screen, `WorkbenchMenu`,** keeps nothing, as a crafting table keeps nothing: the grid and
  the weapon slot go back to the player on closing.
- **The grid** finds recipes of a new type, `rangedweaponsmod:assembly` (`AssemblyRecipe`, shaped
  and shapeless). It matches as the crafting table does, over vanilla's `ShapedRecipePattern` and
  NeoForge's shapeless rule. The result is worked out on the server and sent, as the table's is.
- **No crafting table finds these recipes,** and so neither does a warehouse manager, whose pooled
  crafting reads only the table's type. That is the point of "no longer at the crafting station".
- **They are special, in the game's word:** never in the recipe book, never gated by an unlock or
  by the limited-crafting rule. The blueprints say so in their rep invariant: a bench blueprint
  has no unlocks, a table blueprint at least one.
- **EMI** shows them under "Weapons Workbench", with the bench as the workstation, and its "+"
  fills the bench's grid (`integration/emi/WorkbenchEmiPlugin`, loaded only when EMI is).

**Where each recipe is made is the blueprint's.** `Blueprint` carries a `Station`, TABLE or BENCH,
and `ModRecipes` writes each blueprint at its own station.

| At the bench | At the table |
|---|---|
| every gun and the launcher | the four rounds |
| every part | the rocket |
| every magazine | the weapons workbench |
| the lock-on chip | |

**The fitting panel** is a weapon slot and, under a weapon that takes one, a chip slot. The chip
slot is a view of the launcher: what it shows is the chip carried on the launcher's stack (the
component `rangedweaponsmod:chip`), and what goes in or out is written straight onto the launcher,
as `MagazineMenu`'s slots are a view of a magazine.
- A chip goes on and comes off whole, its wear and any name with it.
- A launcher taken out takes its chip along.
- Which fittings a weapon takes is `Fittings`, today the launcher and the chip. A later fitting (a
  scope, a suppressor) is another entry and another slot, not another screen.
- The launcher gets no screen of its own. The bench took the job the player asked a launcher
  screen for, and rockets keep loading with R from pockets and bags.

**The lock-on chip,** `rangedweaponsmod:lock_on_chip`, is made at the bench: gold nuggets in the
corners, redstone above and below, quartz either side of a comparator.
- **It holds 8 guided launches** (`domain/Chip`). As the chip's durability, they show on its bar
  and on the launcher's tooltip.
- **It is a component value, compared by value.** The chip on a launcher is `FittedChip`, an item
  stack copied in and out and compared with `ItemStack.matches`. An `ItemStack` compares by
  identity, and a menu that saw a changed component on every comparison would resend the slot
  every tick.
- **No chip, no seeker.** `Seeking.tick` keeps the seeker idle unless `LauncherItem.canLock`. The
  client's `SeekReport` searches nothing. The raised sight shows "No lock-on chip" under the
  crosshair in place of its ring, and the rocket flies straight.
- **Wear.** A guided launch (`LauncherWeapon.fire`, when the lock the launch spends held) wears the
  chip by one. The last charge burns it out: the sound of a breaking tool, "Lock-on chip burnt out"
  on the action bar, and no chip. Straight launches, and locks never fired, cost nothing.
- **Creative needs nothing,** the standing rule: it locks with no chip and wears none.
- **A launcher worn out by its 300 shots hands its chip back** (`PlayerGunnery.fire`), read before
  the break, since a broken stack shows no components.

**The art is generated** by `devtools/art/workbench_art.py`, run from `build.py`: the bench's four
faces, the screen's background with the chip slot's frame beside it, the chip's icon, and the
fitting slots' empty icons in vanilla's grey.

**The network version** goes from "4" to "5", for the new menu (memory `payload-version-bump`).

## Why

- **A recipe type of the bench's own.** A subclass of the table's recipes would still be a crafting
  recipe to everything that asks, the recipe book's categories and the warehouse's pool among them,
  and would answer at a crafting table. A type of its own answers at the bench only.
- **The station on the blueprint.** The catalogue stays the one source: the recipe files, their
  unlocks, the tests and the server's answers all follow from it.
- **The chip slot as a view of the launcher.** There is then no container to empty and nothing to
  lose or duplicate on any close: the chip is always on the launcher or in a hand, never in
  between.
- **Wear as durability.** The game already draws it, and a player reads a bar going down.
- **The chip back from a broken launcher.** The chip is the dear part of a guided shot. Losing it
  with a tool's normal wear would punish the player twice.

## Rejected

- **A screen on the launcher opened by sneak and use,** the first plan, superseded by Rusty's
  bench.
- **Subclassing vanilla's shaped and shapeless recipes** for the bench. They would stay crafting
  recipes (above).
- **A vanilla recipe book in the bench.** Its book types are an enum, and EMI, in the pack, does
  the job.
- **A server config for the chip's charges.** Durability is fixed when the item registers, before
  a server config loads. The number is `Chip.CHARGES`, eight.
- **Moving ammunition to the bench.** Rusty kept it at the table, where the warehouse can craft it
  in bulk.

## Consequences

- Guns, parts, magazines and chips no longer craft at a crafting table, nor through a warehouse
  manager.
- Launchers already on the box carry no chip after the update: they fire straight until one is
  fitted.
- Vanilla's generic repair recipe still combines two damaged guns, or two chips, at a crafting
  table; that is the game's, for everything with durability, and not assembly.

## Verified

- **JUnit:**
  - `ChipTest`: fresh, middle, last charge, a single-charge chip, invalid charges and wear.
  - `BlueprintTest`'s stations: a bench recipe has no unlocks, a table recipe at least one, no
    station is refused, and a bench recipe has no advancement.
  - `BlueprintsTest`: every gun, launcher, part, magazine and chip at the bench, every round, the
    rocket and the bench at the table; no two recipes at one station answering one grid (mirrored
    too, and a shapeless one against anything with its ingredients), the check shown to bite; the
    bench's and the chip's recipes.
  - 190 in all.
- **GameTests:**
  - `WorkbenchGameTests`, 7 tests on a server player with real clicks: a rifle at the bench and
    nothing of the same grid at a crafting table; rounds at the table and none at the bench; the
    hand-back on closing; a chip fitted riding the launcher and coming off with its wear; the slot
    rules; shift-click's routing; the block opening the bench when used with a gun in hand and
    dropping itself when broken.
  - 6 chip tests in `LauncherGameTests`: no chip, no seeker, and a chip fitted locks; a straight
    launch spends nothing and a guided one one; the last charge burns the chip out and the seeker
    idles after; creative; the chip back from a worn-out launcher.
  - `CraftingGameTests` holds every recipe to its station and its unlock.
  - 90 in all.
- **Mutations:** each new rule was run once against a mutation, and its tests failed. Nine
  mutations ran in three batches (`devtools/verification/release-2.12.0.md`):
  - the seeker ungated;
  - no chip back;
  - the weapon kept on closing;
  - no wear;
  - the chip slot writing nothing;
  - wear on every launch;
  - creative needing a chip;
  - shift-click ignoring chips;
  - bench recipes typed as crafting.
- **The workbench booth** (`-PboothWorkbench`, EMI copied in for it): 17 checks pass, and the frames
  were judged by eye. They show:
  - the bench beside the crafting and smithing tables at 3×;
  - the chipless hint;
  - the screen empty, with a launcher, and fitted;
  - EMI's bench page, with all 20 bench recipes listed;
  - EMI's "+" filling the grid until the bench offers a rifle;
  - the fitted sight's ring.
- **Recipe collisions:** `collisions.py` checks the 26 recipes against 5,498 in the pack's 115 jars
  plus vanilla's, and finds none.
